## Decide from the user's chair

Product-owner rule, 2026-09-22: decide anything that is yours to decide from the chair of the person using the product, a researcher, clinician or student waiting on an answer. Not the engineer's chair, the reviewer's or the plan's.

Ask, in order:

- What does the person typing the question see today?
- What would they want instead?
- Which option gives them that soonest, and at what cost?
- Would they forgive the trade-off, or feel deceived by it?

Then decide, and state the decision in words they would use ("a question about one gene will never show records for a different gene"), never only in engineering terms.

- Honesty beats polish.
- A refusal says what to type next.
- Speed is a feature.
- A confident wrong record is worse than a missing one.
- Order work by what the user feels first.

Ask the owner only when two options serve two kinds of user differently and the owner has not said which comes first. Full rule with its three-state permissions: `.claude/rules-reference/decide-from-the-users-chair.md`.

The test: if I were the person typing the question, is this what I would want, and did I say the decision in words they would use?
