Orientation

Overview

A one-read orientation to System 3: what it is, why it is different, and what v1 builds. Distilled from the locked PRD and the locked technical specification. Strategic memo, last updated 2026-07-25.

What it is

System 3 is a biomedical research partner. You ask a plain-language question, and it returns a cited, cross-database answer where every claim links back to a real, resolvable NCBI source record. It reaches across the NCBI knowledge graph and the live NCBI and enrichment APIs, through hard guardrails, and assembles what a researcher would otherwise gather by hand. It delivers cited, cross-database synthesis that a general AI tool cannot produce or cite.

Why it exists

It sits between the two failures above. The moat is data plus provenance: cited, deterministic, cross-database synthesis over the NCBI graph and APIs. That is the one thing a general tool cannot copy.

How it works

Every query runs one five-step agent loop and reaches three data layers.

The five-step agent loop, with Act reaching all three data layers.

  • The agent loop: Guardrail validates and blocks unsafe input, Think classifies the query, Plan decomposes it into tool calls, Act runs them, Write synthesizes a cited answer.
  • The three layers: Layer 1 is the knowledge graph (115M nodes, 693M edges), the fast connected map across databases. Layer 2 is the live NCBI APIs, the source of truth for current values. Layer 3 is the enrichment APIs (PubTator3, LitVar2, ClinicalTrials.gov).
  • One core, many surfaces: a single agent core emits one typed event stream, and six thin adapters render it (Web UI, REST plus SSE, GraphQL, MCP server, KGX export, CLI).
  • Cost and safety by construction: strong models plan and write while cheap models fetch and read, and the model never sees a raw untrusted payload. Every claim is cite-or-refuse: it ties to a real source, or the system says it could not find the answer and stops.

What v1 delivers

  • Seven flagship questions: the moat-seven competency questions v1 must answer, from a CNV region to cited ACMG-relevant evidence, to a Salmonella isolate to its outbreak cluster and resistance genes, to a paper to all of its linked data.
  • Seven tools: cypher_query for the graph, ncbi_efetch and ncbi_dbsnp for NCBI records and variants, pubtator_annotate and litvar2_lookup for literature enrichment, and pathogen_detection and clinicaltrials_search for the outbreak and clinical-trial paths.
  • Two hard guarantees: cite-or-refuse, so no claim ships uncited, and assemble the evidence never the verdict, so the system cites clinical evidence but never diagnoses, classifies pathogenicity, or prioritizes a variant. A human decides.

How we know it works

  • The moat test selects the questions: a candidate qualifies only if no strong general tool answers it with real, resolvable citations. That keeps the bar honest against 2026 tools.
  • An offline eval gate runs before any answer feature ships: an 8-point rubric with cite-or-refuse enforced, scored for reliability, not a single lucky pass.
  • An online feedback loop grows the question set from real usage, turning what people actually ask into better routing.
  • The milestone ladder frames success as four climbing gates: each tool works alone, sources join across the layers, answers are cited and credible to a subject-matter expert, and the result is reusable through every delivery format.

Where it goes

The end state is a research partner, not a chatbot. The work that once took a researcher 500 sources over weeks becomes a 30-minute cited conversation that feeds the next experiment and drops into a paper with its references already attached. Session memory holds the research arc, and per-user context compounds the moat, while the cited data underneath stays the real differentiator.

Problem statement and the wedge

A biomedical researcher with a real question, such as what is known about a variant, an isolate, or a paper's linked data, has to cross several NCBI databases by hand: Gene, then ClinVar, then PubMed, then dbVar, then MedGen, copying identifiers between them. It is slow, it is easy to miss a link, and the result is not assembled anywhere.

A general AI tool answers instantly, but it answers from training priors. For biomedical work that is worse than slow, it is untrustworthy: the tool produces a fluent answer with citations that are often wrong or invented, and the researcher cannot tell which. A confident wrong answer is more dangerous than no answer.

System 3 is the wedge between those two failures. A user asks one plain-language question. The agent queries across three data layers (the knowledge graph, live NCBI APIs, and enrichment APIs) through hard guardrails, and returns a cited answer where every claim links to a real, resolvable source record.

The differentiation anchor is data plus provenance: cited, deterministic, cross-database synthesis over the NCBI graph and APIs. Per-user personalization compounds the moat because it fuses with that data and the user's own cited research history, but personalization alone is copyable and is not the differentiator.

Two hard guarantees

Cite-or-refuse

Every claim ties to a specific retrieved source, or the system refuses and stops. No claim ships uncited.

Assemble the evidence, never the verdict

The system reports cited evidence and clinical significance from source records. It never gives personal medical advice, diagnosis, treatment, a pathogenicity classification, or a variant prioritization. A human decides; the system assembles and cites.

v1 scope

The input stays chat-simple for every persona: one plain-language question, no forms, no query syntax, no database picking. Eleven personas were identified; the wedge-strong ones drive v1.

  • Literature researchers, sequence data users, geneticists, bioinformaticians
  • Epidemiologists and public health, the strongest wedge persona in the roadmap usage evidence
  • Cross-database journey users
  • AI agents and MCP and LLM consumers, an emerging 2025 to 2026 signal
  • Served with a clinical-safety boundary: clinicians and genetic counselors. The system assembles cited evidence for them and never renders a verdict.

Every query follows the same five-step agent loop shown in the overview above. Guardrail validates and blocks; Think classifies the query and resolves free-text terms to CURIEs; Plan decomposes into tool calls; Act executes them, graph-first with API correction; Write synthesizes a cited research brief with placeholder markers the harness verifies.

UI experience

The surface is a workspace home, not a bare chat box: a plain-language input, saved queries, and suggested competency questions. The answer is a structured, provenance-forward research brief. Streaming is the default (time-to-first-token under one second), a stop button works throughout the loop, citation chips render inline and link to resolvable source records, and an optional expander reveals the curated reasoning narrative.

Delivery formats

Five formats are in scope so the same cited answer is reusable, the G4 outcome on the milestone ladder: Web UI (the primary surface), a GraphQL API for structured programmatic access, an MCP server so other agents can consume System 3, a KGX export scoped to the existing graph, and a CLI agent. The REST plus SSE surface carries the chat streaming; GraphQL carries structured access; both share one auth and one tool set.

Out of scope for v1

  • Compute tools: no BLAST, no sequence-similarity search, no VCF ingestion (the fast-follow set, Q2, Q7, Q9)
  • Segmental-duplication overlap for Q1, deferred to the fast-follow with a UCSC source
  • External non-NCBI knowledge-graph federation; v1 federation is exactly the three data layers
  • Model distillation and fusion or ensemble model panels, both v2 optimizations
  • The automated mining half of the online feedback loop; v1 ships capture plus manual review plus hand-promotion
  • Full Section 508 and WCAG audit, and enterprise security (IAM, session expiry, access review), both the production track
  • Sub-query decomposition for deep research, triggered later by a failure rate above 20 percent on that query class

The moat test

The moat test selects and tiers competency questions, and it outranks persona coverage and raw usage frequency. A candidate qualifies only if it passes two binary gates, provenance (every claim links to a source record) and determinism (the answer is reproducible given the data state at query time), and then scores high on a ranking dimension: no general-tool equivalent. The candidate is run against a panel of the strongest general tools, a frontier chat LLM, a retrieval-backed answer engine, and plain web search. It passes only if none of them produces the correct answer with verifiable, resolvable citations. Full mechanics, the tier mapping, and the coverage metric that pairs with it are in the evaluation playbook below.

The one-core, six-surface model

System 3 is one agent core wrapped in thin, surface-specific adapters. The core owns the five-step loop, the three-layer retrieval model, and a single typed event contract. Every delivery surface subscribes to that contract; none re-implement agent logic.

Four streaming adapters subscribe to the live event stream (Web UI, REST plus SSE, MCP, CLI). GraphQL and KGX export reuse the same tools and layers in a request or response and batch shape instead.

The agent loop

Each step has exactly one job. No step reaches past its neighbor.

StepJob
GuardrailValidate input, reject prompt injection and off-topic or medical-advice requests, check rate and cost pre-caps. A cheap non-LLM pre-filter runs before any model call.
ThinkClassify query shape (lookup, single-hop, multi-hop, aggregate, exploratory), resolve free-text terms to CURIEs, ask one clarifying question on ambiguity.
PlanDecompose into tool calls, select the model tier per step, produce a structured database-neutral plan. Never writes Cypher.
ActExecute tool calls, independent ones in parallel. cypher_query generates and validates its own Cypher internally.
WriteSynthesize a cited research brief from structured findings only, never a raw retrieved document.

Three data layers

LayerContentsLatencyFreshness
Layer 1AGE knowledge graph on Hetzner: 115M nodes, 693M edges, 10 concept labels, 14 edge predicates, from 5 NCBI databasesSub-10ms typed queriesPeriodic snapshot
Layer 2Live NCBI APIs: E-utilities, Datasets API v2, Variation Services, PubChem100 to 500msAlways current
Layer 3Enrichment APIs: PubTator3, LitVar2, LitSense, ClinicalTrials.gov200ms to 2sAlways current

One-tool-one-layer discipline: no tool call spans two layers. A question needing both graph context and a live record is two tool calls in the Plan step's list, never one tool that internally hops layers. On disagreement between a graph result and a live API result, the live value wins for currency, the graph value stays for traversal breadth, and both are cited with their own layer tag rather than one silently overwriting the other.

The harness and model tiers

The harness is an in-process module (v1) that owns tier resolution, cost accounting, timeout enforcement, and the coordinator-worker split. A strong-enough tier plans and writes; a cheap tier does bounded reads; the split is drawn at the untrusted-content boundary, not at every tool result.

TierPurposeRuns which step
GuardFast, cheap input validation and classificationGuardrail, Think, and the Act coordinator-worker reader pass
PlanMid-range query decomposition and tool selectionPlan, and cypher_query's internal Cypher-generation call
SynthStrongest model, final synthesis and citation assembly onlyWrite, once per query

Structured data (a Cypher row, a Datasets API field) passes straight from Act to Write with no reader pass. Free text (a PubMed abstract, an annotation span, a record description) routes through an isolated Guard-tier reader first, scoped to Read plus the one API that produced the payload, never Write, never able to call another tool. Synth never sees a raw record, only the reader's structured findings or pass-through structured fields. Model identity per tier is a config value resolved at Phase 6 by model-bench, a config change, never a code change.

Every tool is a read-plus-one-source untrusted-source reader: it can call its own source and nothing else, never another tool, never a write path. Every schema carries maxLength on strings, maxItems on arrays, and a host-pinned source_url regex, per the multi-agent pipeline gate.

Event and provenance schema

The spine is one agent core that exposes a single typed event stream, versioned v1. Every event shares one envelope: type, version, trace_id, a monotonic seq, a timestamp, and a payload. The surface never sees a raw tool payload, a raw LLM completion, or raw chain-of-thought; it only ever consumes this stream.

The provenance type

Every citation, whether it lands in the UI as a chip, in the SSE stream, in an MCP response, or in a paper-facing export, carries the same one type.

  • source, source_id, source_url, layer: the base fields locked by CLAUDE.md's citations section.
  • field, claim_text: which record field the claim rests on and the exact text it grounds.
  • evidence_kind: primary assertion, derived summary, literature mention, or external annotation.
  • assertion_confidence: asserted, hedged, or contested, populated by a fixed lookup table or hedge lexicon, never model judgment.
  • population_ancestry_context: nullable population or ancestry data, rendered as "not specified" rather than guessed when absent.
  • license: public domain US government, publisher-copyright abstract-only, or unspecified.

Every source_url is validated against a host-pinned regex before it is attached to a citation, for example ^https://(www\.|pubmed\.)?ncbi\.nlm\.nih\.gov/. A citation always resolves to the human-facing record page, never the raw eutils. or api. fetch host the tool actually called.

Cite-or-refuse grounding

After Write returns narrative text and before any token reaches a surface, the harness parses each inline marker, resolves it against the findings list Synth was given, and accepts a claim as grounded only by exact or substring match after normalization, lowercase, whitespace-collapsed, punctuation-stripped. No embedding similarity, no LLM-judged closeness. Any clause that fails is stripped, marker included; if stripping removes the query's core ask entirely, the whole answer is discarded and the refuse path returns instead.

The trust signal

A deterministic decision table over risk tier, whether a claim is grounded, and (for high-risk claims) whether independent sources triangulate, yields one of four outcomes.

Risk tierGroundedTriangulationOutcome
Low or highFalsen/aRefuse
LowTrueNot evaluatedAnswer
HighTrueConcordantAnswer
HighTrueDiscordantFlag
HighTrueInsufficient (fewer than 2 independent sources)Ask

High risk covers ClinVar clinical-significance and review-status fields, OMIM mechanistic mappings, cause or treat relationships extracted from literature, and AMR-to-clinical-outcome fields. A refuse outcome always emits a validated NCBI cross-database fallback link rather than a dead end, and is treated as an intentional content-safety outcome, not a system failure.

Key safety gates

Read-only, enforced three times

The Guardrail rejects write-seeking requests outright, the cypher_query validator rejects any write-shaped clause before execution, and the database connection uses a kg_reader role with no write grant. No single layer carries the whole guarantee.

Prompt-injection defense

Retrieved content is never formatted into a system or developer-role message. Only the isolated reader ever sees untrusted free text, has no tool-calling ability, and its own output is schema-validated before Synth sees it.

Cost caps, safety-critical by construction

Per-query ($0.10), per-user-daily (100 queries), system-wide-daily ($10), and per-step timeouts. The harness is the sole enforcement point; no tool or surface enforces its own cap.

Secrets in env only

The Hetzner connection string, NCBI API key, OpenRouter key, and every other credential live in environment variables or a secrets manager, never in code, prompts, logs, or generated docs.

Every Layer 2 and Layer 3 access is logged with what was queried and under what authorization: an append-only audit log, full LangSmith traces linked by trace_id, and the PostgreSQL interactions table, each serving a different audience.

The 8-point scoring rubric

The offline evaluation gate runs before any answer-generation feature ships. Each criterion scores 0, 1, or 2.

Criterion012
Intent understandingMisreads the taskMostly right, misses constraintsRestates task and constraints
Entity normalizationNo stable entitiesSome normalizedAll key entities normalized with IDs
Database routingWrong or shallowHits some databasesHits all required or explains unavailable
Evidence qualityUnsupported claimsPartial citationsClaims tied to source records and IDs
Cross-database synthesisLists records onlySome synthesisExplains how records connect
Freshness and versioningNo dates or versionsPartial contextClear date, version, assembly context
Safety and limitsOverclaimsSome caveatsSeparates evidence, inference, and limits
Output usabilityHard to reuseHuman-readable onlyHuman-readable plus structured IDs

Pass threshold: 13 of 16. Any single run hits a hard-fail, and fails the gate regardless of total score, on: provenance scored 0 (a claim with no source), safety and limits scored 0 on a clinical or pathogenicity question (a rendered verdict), or missing assembly or version context on a coordinate or sequence question. Cite-or-refuse lives here too: zero retrieval plus a correct refusal scores as pass, not fail. Abstain-as-pass is an explicit rubric outcome.

Pass metrics: pass@k and pass/fail/abstain

The rubric grades one run into one outcome: pass (at least 13 of 16, no hard-fail), fail (any hard-fail, or under 13), or abstain (returned the refusal string). The eval-harness pass@k and pass^k metrics aggregate those per-run outcomes across k samples. They compose, they do not compete.

TargetLevelBar
Hard-failsEvery run, no exceptionspass^k = 100 percent (no fabrication and no verdict, ever)
Quality (13 of 16)Can it produce the answer at allpass@3 as the floor
Quality (13 of 16)Does it do so consistentlypass^3 at least 90 percent

A fabrication is never acceptable; quality is high but not required to be perfect. This matches a biomedical setting where a confident wrong answer is worse than no answer. Determinism runs against pinned fixtures, with a freshness-window allowance for live-API questions.

How tool and layer combinations are judged

The moat test is the primary bar for selecting and tiering competency questions, ahead of persona coverage and usage frequency. It runs two binary gates and one ranking dimension.

The moat test: two binary gates, then a ranking dimension that tiers the survivors.

  • Provenance: every claim links to a source record, or the question is disqualified, not low-scored.
  • Deterministic: the answer is reproducible given the data state at query time.
  • No general-tool equivalent: the candidate runs against a panel of the strongest general tools (a frontier chat LLM, a retrieval-backed answer engine, plain web search) and passes only if none produces the answer with verifiable, resolvable citations.

A second, orthogonal instrument, the coverage metric, answers a different question: of the graph's concept and predicate space, how much does the eval set actually exercise. It is a diagnostic, never a gate, because the moat set is deliberately narrow and cross-database-biased. On the first pass, the seven touch roughly 5 of 10 concept labels and 3 of 14 edge predicates in the graph, but reach across roughly 9 API databases outside the graph (dbVar, OMIM, GTR, Pathogen Detection, SRA, BioProject, GEO, Assembly, PubChem), since four of the seven questions are Layer 2 dominant and barely touch Layer 1.

The golden-dataset approach

The moat seven are the initial offline eval set. The Phase 4 golden dataset of 50 queries is the expansion; every acceptance-criteria table measures against that fixed set. Beyond the seven, an online feedback loop grows the question set from real usage: capture every interaction, mine and cluster periodically, review with a human gate, apply a trigger rule (a recurring, moat-passing, not-yet-covered cluster becomes a candidate question), and promote an approved question as a new few-shot routing example plus a new eval case. In v1, capture, review, and promotion ship with a human in the loop; automated mining is a fast-follow once enough data is captured to cluster. The LLM-judge, where used, is always a filter upstream of the human, never the final say: promotion always needs a human approval, because it changes routing.
