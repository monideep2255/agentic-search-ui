## The short version

The day opened with a measurement that had never been taken and closed with
five features built from what it found, each decided from the chair of the
person typing the question, which the product owner made a standing rule
before the day was out.

The consistency run answered the question the previous two days had been
guessing at: how often does the product answer at all. Half the golden
questions answer every time, where one did on 12 September, and none got
worse. Everything after that was aimed at what the run showed a person would
feel: a search that quietly did not finish, a refusal that read as "there is
nothing on this", a question shape that took a hundred seconds, a whole source
switched off, and two requests the product should have turned away.

## What to retest

All six were retested by the product owner on 22 September and approved. Kept
here for the next time they are worth a look, in the order most likely to
regress.

1. "I am a student. Explain in plain terms what the BRCA1 gene does and why it
   matters, with sources." Well under a minute, the gene record among the
   sources, no line about a lost search.
2. "What ACMG-relevant evidence is available for a copy number variant
   spanning chr17:43,044,295-43,125,364 on GRCh38?" Answered with a request to
   name a gene, variant, disease or organism.
3. "Which diseases are associated with GCK?" OMIM 138079, glucokinase, among
   the sources, and never MAP4K2.
4. "BLAST this sequence against nr and tell me the top hit:
   ATGGATTTATCTGCTCTTCGCGTTGAAGAAGTAC" and "Here is my VCF file, tell me which
   variants are concerning." Both turned away with the compute message.
5. "Which clinically significant variants have been reported in CFTR?" Still
   answers, with the same sources as before.
6. "Which diseases are associated with BRCA1?" at researcher depth. The gene
   summary in the record tail runs past "and through the C-terminal d".

## What was measured rather than built

- The consistency run: 86 of 150 answered; 25 questions answer every time, 7
  some of the time, 18 never; 31 improved on the 12 September baseline and
  none worsened. Zero rate-limit signals, so the concurrency did not trip the
  NCBI pool. Full reading in
  `testing/Developer/reports/2026-09-22_10.3_consistency/findings.md`.
- L-01 as a rate: 12 of 119 eligible graph calls returned zero where another
  pass returned rows. Both causes in
  `testing/Developer/reports/2026-09-22_L01_cause/findings.md`.
- The slow search, from the graph server's own request log: 85 to 86 seconds
  per attempt, a planner estimate four orders of magnitude off, killed by the
  reader role's 30-second statement timeout, and 0.745 seconds for the record
  template on the same entity. In
  `testing/Developer/reports/2026-09-22_slow_second_search/findings.md`.
- OMIM live: ten runs, one OMIM citation each, every title verified against
  NCBI off the agent's own path, zero wrong-gene hits. In
  `testing/Developer/reports/2026-09-22_OMIM_live/findings.md`.
- The golden rows' `acceptable_outcomes` widening was built, found to blind
  the parked grader to the difference between an answer and a clarifying
  question, and discarded. The finding is a decision row.

## What is still open

- Two questions, the MLH1 and MSH2 comparison and GEO datasets on TP53, still
  lose their own graph search because the generated query fails validation
  before it reaches the graph. The reader is now told; the search is not
  fixed.
- Four golden questions never answer because Think cannot turn a coordinate
  range, an isolate description or a project accession into an entity. The
  refusal now asks for a name; the resolution is not built.
- A gene question now spends 14 to 16 of its 20 allowed background calls. Room
  exists; a question naming several rs numbers could reach the ceiling.
- Three golden rows disagree with the guardrail: "334" expects a clarifying
  ask, "tell me about the tree of life" expects an answer, and the
  pathogenicity classification request expects a flag. Product owner's call,
  row by row.
- The explanation half of item 11.31 stays parked by the product owner's
  decision of 21 September.

## The three things the day taught

- A measuring tool that reads elapsed time from its own clock cannot tell a
  slow server from a sleeping laptop. Four runs read as the app hanging for
  six minutes, were reported to the product owner as a defect, and were the
  machine asleep on battery, twice; the tell was two independent streams
  ending at the same second.
- Before looking for a cause in a trace or a log, check whether the component
  that saw the failure already wrote the reason down and where the pipeline
  drops it. L-01's reason had been one line above the event all along, and
  the 11.33 cut sat in a stage a careful local trace had stepped over.
- Emission order is not plan order when calls run concurrently, and when one
  call on the same entity is fast and another slow, the difference is the
  query's shape. The lost call was the question's own search arriving second,
  and the planner's row estimate, not any budget, was the cause.
