## Verdict and recommendation

The opening line every answer shows today is `answer_summary_sentence` in `src/system_03_search_agent/synthesis/answer_layout.py`, called from `core/graph.py`'s write node and always emitted first in `_answer_tokens` (`if summary_sentence: sentence_token(summary_sentence, emphasize=True)`, ahead of every model sentence). It counts records and names a few, but it never answers the question, because it is built from retrieval bookkeeping, not from the field the question actually asked for.

Reading 15 saved answers against their own cited records shows two different problems hiding under one symptom:

- Roughly half the sample (BRCA1 and BRCA2 diseases, Lynch syndrome, Marfan syndrome's clinical features, carcinoma trials) has a real answer sitting one or two sentences below the opening line, already grounded, already cited, already accepted by the cite-or-refuse gate. The fix here is purely about ORDER.
- The other half (TP53 orthologs, CFTR papers, PMID MeSH terms, rs334, the E. coli ESBL isolates) has NO answer anywhere in the cited records, because the records shown carry no organism field, no title field, no gene field, or, for rs334, do not appear to be the requested variant at all. No reordering fixes these; a good first sentence here has to say plainly that the shown records do not carry what was asked, which is a different and equally honest thing to say than a bare count.

Recommendation: design C, the classifier-gated lead (see [Designs](#designs)). A guard-tier call, already the cheapest tier in the harness and already precedented by the 2026-09-23 sentence-fidelity check, decides per run whether a model-grounded sentence answers the question and should lead, or whether nothing does and the code-built sentence should lead instead, extended to name the field it is missing rather than only a count. This is chosen over the two named alternatives because:

- Design A (model always leads) has nothing to lead with on exactly the runs in this sample where the model's own prose was discarded outright (structured fallback: G-007, G-016, G-019, G-021, G-024), so it silently falls back to today's behavior for the hardest cases and can mislead on multi-part questions where the model's first sentence answers only one facet of five.
- Design B alone (a code-built sentence picked by question shape) risks becoming a hardcoded decision: choosing which field answers "phenotypic features" versus "orthologs" by matching the question's wording is exactly the pattern DECISIONS.md (2026-09-24) forbids. Design C keeps design B's sentence-composition mechanism but moves the LEAD decision to the classifier, never to a string match on the question.
- Design C also matches the direction already in flight this phase (8.6, "Jev makes every choice"): a classifier decides, code only verifies.

Two things worth naming out loud from this table, because they change what any design has to tolerate:

- A genuinely good first sentence is sometimes a stated gap ("not answerable from these records"), not a claim. Five of the fifteen (G-016, G-019, G-021, G-024, G-035) have no field in their cited records that answers what was asked, so any design that forces a first sentence to always assert an answer will either fabricate (a cite-or-refuse violation) or keep failing quietly.
- Several of the fifteen (G-011, G-013, G-022) already have a model-written sentence sitting one slot below the code-built line that is a better opening line as written today, with no new code needed beyond moving it up.

## Designs

### Design A: the model's first grounded sentence leads

`_answer_tokens` stops always emitting `summary_sentence` first. When `model_grounding` produced at least one kept sentence (the ordinary path, not structured fallback), that sentence leads; the code-built summary sentence, unchanged and still fully cited, moves to right after the model's prose as a record-count line rather than the answer's opening claim. When the structured-fallback path ran (the model's own prose failed grounding entirely), nothing changes: the code-built sentence stays the lead, exactly as today.

Failure mode: on this sample, 5 of 15 runs (G-007, G-016, G-019, G-021, G-024, all "structured fallback could not be verified" runs) have no model sentence at all, so this design silently reverts to today's line for exactly the runs the product owner is most likely to notice, since those are the ones with nothing better to say. On multi-part questions (G-002 asks for the gene record, conditions, variants, genetic tests and literature) the model's first sentence answers only the facet it happened to write about first, and reads as a complete answer when four of five asked-for facets have not appeared yet. Nothing in this design checks whether the model's first sentence is actually responsive to the question; it is just "whatever came out first."

### Design B: a code-built answer sentence composed from the records the question asked about

`answer_summary_sentence` (or a sibling function) is extended so that when the ENTITY TYPE the retrieval plan resolved carries a known descriptive field, for example `clinical_features` on a MedGen record, the sentence opens with that field's values directly: "Marfan syndrome's clinical features in MedGen include aortic regurgitation [2], arachnodactyly [3] and 7 others," used whenever the model's own first sentence fails the grounding or fidelity check. When no cited record on that entity type carries a descriptive field (the variant, article and gene-record shapes in this sample), it falls back to today's count sentence, worded to say what field is missing rather than staying silent about the gap.

Failure mode: the field this sentence opens with has to be chosen somehow, and choosing it by matching words in the question ("phenotypic features" implies read `clinical_features`) is a hardcoded decision on the exact pattern DECISIONS.md forbade on 2026-09-24: a rule shaped around a specific question's wording. To stay compliant, the field has to come from a property the Plan step already decided (which field answers this entity type's question), which is new plan-step surface, not a small patch to `answer_layout.py`, and until that surface exists this design's improvement is gated entirely on which entity types happen to carry a descriptive field already, which is uneven: MedGen and Disease records often do, variant and article records in this sample never do.

### Design C: a guard-tier classifier decides which sentence leads (recommended)

Keep both ingredients above, but move the LEAD decision to a guard-tier model call instead of hardcoding it in `graph.py`. After grounding accepts the model's prose, or after the structured-fallback path runs, one guard-tier call is shown only the question and the first one or two already-grounded, already-cited sentences (never the full answer, so the stable prefix in `prompt-cache-discipline` stays untouched and only the dynamic suffix grows) and asked a single yes-or-no: does this sentence state a fact that responds to what was asked. Yes: that sentence leads, and the code-built summary sentence (design B's version, extended with the descriptive-field lookup where the plan step supplies one) follows immediately after as the record-count line. No, or no model sentence survived grounding, or the classifier call itself fails, times out, or the query is out of cost budget: the code-built sentence leads, exactly as it does today. Nothing about cite-or-refuse changes, because only sentences the grounding pass has already accepted are candidates for the vote; a hallucinated sentence can never win the vote and lead, matching the same fail-closed, code-decides-first shape as the 2026-09-23 rewording exception in `production-standards`.

Failure mode: one more guard-tier call per answered or ask run, at guard-tier's cost and latency, which is the cheapest tier in the harness but is not free; and the visible first sentence is no longer perfectly deterministic across identical retrievals, since the model's own prose (the candidate the classifier is voting on) can vary in wording run to run even when the same records are cited. That is a real cost against the golden consistency run, which needs an explicit answer to "does 'consistent' mean the same records and the same verdict, or the same wording," before this ships, because today it is implicitly the latter (the code-built line never varies) and this design makes it the former.

## Acceptance test

Questions to run: the 15 questions in this report, rerun three times each the way the phase 8.2 golden run already does, plus the full 50-query golden set through `/eval-harness`, so every one of the six shapes named in this brief (gene, disease, variant, literature, trials, accession) is covered, including the shapes this sample found with no answerable field (G-016, G-019, G-021, G-024, G-035 shapes).

What the first sentence must do:

- State a fact that responds to the literal question, when the cited records carry one, using only what is in a cited record.
- When no cited record carries the fact the question asked for, say so in the first sentence rather than only counting records, in the same honest terms as the five gap rows in the table above.
- Carry at least one citation marker, and never carry a fact absent from the record that marker points to. This check is deterministic, per `production-standards`'s AI answer grounding gate, never a fuzzy similarity score.
- Read in different words at Plain language versus Researcher depth for the same run, per item 12.9, while citing the same records and carrying the same markers at both depths.

How the golden run would show it worked: the three-pass consistency run's answered-count floor must not drop, per bossman-mode's golden-run gate. Add one column the golden harness can already compute from `answer_text`'s first sentence: whether it is the code-built "Found N..." line or a model-led line, tracked as a percentage across the 50-query set. Today, per the 2026-09-25 writer bench, that figure is 100% code-built across all four models tested; a working design should show that percentage drop, and the honest-gap runs should be visibly identifiable as "not answerable from these records" wording rather than a silent count sitting where an answer was expected.

How the product review's rubric line 1 would show it worked: the product-reviewer's five-line rubric line 1 (does the first sentence answer the question) should score yes on runs with a real grounded answer among the cited records, such as G-011, G-013 and G-022 above, and should score a distinct "states a gap honestly" outcome, not a plain "no," on runs like G-016, G-019, G-021, G-024 and G-035, where no cited record actually carries what was asked. A design that cannot produce that second, honest outcome and instead forces every run toward a claim is not ready to ship, regardless of how good it looks on the runs that already have an answer to give.
