Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) is a gastrointestinal motility disorder that results from the reflux of stomach contents into the esophagus or oral cavity resulting in symptoms or complications.⁠1 The typical symptoms of GERD are heartburn and regurgitation of gastric contents into the oropharynx.⁠1 The condition affects quality of life and may cause erosive esophagitis, esophageal strictures, and Barrett esophagus, a precursor to esophageal adenocarcinoma.⁠1

Gastroesophageal reflux (GER) in children is very common and refers to the involuntary passage of gastric contents into the esophagus.⁠2 This is often physiological and managed conservatively.⁠2 In contrast, GER disease (GERD) is a less common pathologic process causing troublesome symptoms, which may need medical management.⁠2 The clinical manifestations of GERD in young children are varied and nonspecific prompting the necessity for careful diagnostic evaluation.⁠2

TREATMENT

GERD is a clinical diagnosis and is most effectively treated with proton-pump inhibitors (PPIs).⁠1 Long-term use of PPIs is associated with bone fractures, chronic renal disease, acute renal disease, community-acquired pneumonia, and Clostridium difficile intestinal infection.⁠1
