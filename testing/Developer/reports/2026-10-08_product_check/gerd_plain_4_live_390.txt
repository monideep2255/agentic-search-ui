Gastroesophageal reflux disease, or GERD, happens when stomach contents flow back up into the esophagus or mouth, causing symptoms or complications.⁠1 The typical symptoms are heartburn and regurgitation of gastric contents into the oropharynx.⁠1 GERD can affect quality of life and may lead to erosive esophagitis, esophageal strictures, and Barrett esophagus, which is a precursor to esophageal adenocarcinoma.⁠1 Long-term use of proton-pump inhibitors, the most effective treatment for GERD, is associated with bone fractures, chronic and acute kidney disease, community-acquired pneumonia, and Clostridium difficile intestinal infection.⁠1
