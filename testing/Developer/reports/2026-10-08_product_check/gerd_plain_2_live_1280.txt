The typical symptoms of GERD are heartburn and regurgitation of stomach contents into the throat, and the condition can lead to complications including inflammation of the esophagus, narrowing of the esophagus, and a precancerous change called Barrett esophagus.⁠1 GERD happens when stomach contents flow back into the esophagus or mouth, causing symptoms or complications.⁠1 In children, reflux itself is very common and is often a normal process, while GERD is a less common disease process that causes troublesome symptoms and may need medical treatment.⁠2
