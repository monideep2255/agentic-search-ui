GERD happens when stomach contents flow back up into the esophagus or mouth, causing symptoms or complications [1].

In children, reflux itself is common and often normal, while GERD is a less common disease process that causes troublesome symptoms and may need treatment [2]. The most effective treatment is a class of drugs called proton-pump inhibitors, but using them for a long time carries risks including bone fractures, kidney disease, pneumonia, and intestinal infection [1].
