## Verdict

FAIL against the card's goal as the lead stated it ("list up to 10 papers from that window, each with its title, while the writer still reads five abstracts"). The listing half holds; the reading half does not hold as a person would understand it. "Still reads five abstracts" is true only as a ceiling: the five are chosen by PMID among listed rows, so a windowed answer can read zero (A-119-01, live, cystic fibrosis) and, when it does read five, they are often not the five best-matched papers develop read (A-119-02, live on caffeine, measured on Marfan). Both are worse than develop and both sit inside this card's new code (`_pubmed_abstract_rows`' newest-five selection, `core/graph.py:7987-8003`). This is round one, so no finding sits inside a previous round's fix; A-119-01 and A-119-02 sit inside this phase's new code.

Verified with my own probes (live runs, my own ESearch and efetch calls, the deterministic shaping probe): A-119-01, A-119-02 and their addenda; A-119-04 (both sides live); A-119-05 trust and count lines over 19 answer files; A-119-06 answer lengths against the 8000 cut; A-119-03 and A-119-07 as observed answers.
Read only, not run: that the follow-up routes in A-119-03 and A-119-07 are unchanged by this card (relies on the judge's J-119-E1/E6 and on the window coming only from a picked option, `core/graph.py:4016-4029`); MCP and GraphQL truncation behaviour (read the code, measured only the input length).
Not found: any summary sentence citing a paper whose abstract the writer did not get, or a "From the paper" lead-in naming the wrong paper. Checked on all ten card 119 windowed answers with summaries: every summary marker on a PubMed record pointed at an abstract row, and every lead-in's title matched the cited row's PMID.
