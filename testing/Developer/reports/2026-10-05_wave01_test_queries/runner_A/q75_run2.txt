Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) is a gastrointestinal motility disorder stemming from the reflux of stomach contents into the esophagus or oral cavity, producing symptoms or complications.⁠1

GERD may lead to erosive esophagitis, esophageal strictures, and Barrett esophagus, a precursor to esophageal adenocarcinoma.⁠1

Clinical manifestations in young children are varied and nonspecific.⁠2

TREATMENT AND MANAGEMENT

GERD is most effectively treated with proton-pump inhibitors (PPIs), and clinical approaches include PPI trials as well as specific indications warranting gastroenterology referral.⁠1, 3 Long-term PPI use is associated with bone fractures, chronic and acute renal disease, community-acquired pneumonia, and Clostridium difficile intestinal infection.⁠1
