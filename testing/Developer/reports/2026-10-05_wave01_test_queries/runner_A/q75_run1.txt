GERD is a gastrointestinal motility disorder caused by reflux of stomach contents into the esophagus or oral cavity, producing symptoms or complications.⁠1

ASSOCIATED COMPLICATIONS

GERD may cause erosive esophagitis, esophageal strictures, and Barrett esophagus, the latter being a precursor to esophageal adenocarcinoma.⁠1

GERD is most effectively treated with proton-pump inhibitors, and symptoms potentially attributable to the disease are among those most commonly reported to primary care providers.⁠1, 2 Long-term PPI use is associated with bone fractures, chronic and acute renal disease, community-acquired pneumonia, and Clostridium difficile intestinal infection.⁠1
