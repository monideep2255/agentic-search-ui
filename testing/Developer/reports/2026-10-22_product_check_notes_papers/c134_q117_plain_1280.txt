A group of papers reports on specific spelling mistakes, called mutations, in the CFTR gene found in patients.⁠2, 6, 26

One paper reports on CFTR gene changes in a group of patients with asthma, widened airways, and chronic obstructive lung disease.⁠30

Another reports on how common CFTR mutations are among babies flagged by newborn screening.⁠24 One study looks at how a separate immune-system gene can affect liver disease severity in cystic fibrosis patients.⁠29 A structural paper details the physical connection between a regulatory protein and the tail end of the CFTR protein.⁠28 A further paper looks at CFTR gene changes in Japanese patients with chronic pancreas inflammation.⁠20
