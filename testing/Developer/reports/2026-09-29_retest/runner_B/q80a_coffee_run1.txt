ERGOGENIC EFFECTS ON PERFORMANCE

Aerobic endurance shows the most consistent moderate-to-large benefits, and the effect magnitude is generally greater for aerobic than anaerobic exercise.⁠1, 2 Caffeine does not directly improve maximal oxygen capacity but may permit training at greater power output or longer duration, with effects demonstrated in activities lasting from 60 seconds to 2 hours.⁠3

A critical distinction emerges: caffeine ingested as coffee appears ineffective compared with pure caffeine supplementation.⁠3 Caffeine appears effective in both trained and untrained individuals, and there is no evidence of dehydration, ion imbalance or other adverse effects from pre-exercise ingestion.⁠1, 3

The popular theory that caffeine enhances fat oxidation and spares muscle glycogen has very little support.⁠3

Caffeine consumption reduces total sleep time by 45 minutes and sleep efficiency by 7%, with increased sleep onset latency and wake after sleep onset.⁠4 Separately, moderate coffee consumption is associated with reduced risk of hypertension, type 2 diabetes, myocardial infarction, arrhythmias, heart failure and overall mortality, though many beneficial effects appear independent of caffeine.⁠5 Unfiltered coffee may raise LDL cholesterol, and coffee consumption acutely increases premature ventricular contractions.⁠5
