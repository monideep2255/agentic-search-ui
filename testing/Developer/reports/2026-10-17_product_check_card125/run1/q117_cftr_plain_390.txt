One paper examines a control region of DNA upstream of the CFTR gene, and another investigates how splicing factors can cause a part of CFTR's genetic instructions to be skipped.⁠4, 8 I found 54 published papers related to CFTR, out of 1962 available.⁠1–20

One describes a patient with a D579G mutation who had milder lung and pancreas involvement than expected, and another reports a D513G mutation found in a French patient with a condition affecting the vas deferens.⁠2, 6 The S1235R mutation is the subject of a collaborative study on its frequency and clinical significance.⁠26 Genetic findings in patients with congenital bilateral aplasia of the vas deferens are reported, including six new mutations.⁠3 CFTR expression is also examined in a kidney disease context.⁠16 On the topic of screening and severity, one paper shows that newborn screening for cystic fibrosis prevents severe malnutrition and improves growth, and another reports on how common CFTR mutations are among infants flagged by screening.⁠21, 24 A separate gene that influences liver disease severity in cystic fibrosis patients is also covered.⁠29
