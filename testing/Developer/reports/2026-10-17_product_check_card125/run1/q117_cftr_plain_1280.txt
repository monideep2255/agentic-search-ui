I found 54 published papers related to CFTR, out of 1962 available.⁠1–20

One of these describes a patient with a mutation called D579G who had a milder form of the disease with less lung damage.⁠2 One of these papers reports that a pair of helper molecules called Hdj-2 and Hsc70 assists with the early stages of CFTR production.⁠1 Another shows that a protein called CHIP marks immature CFTR for destruction.⁠22 One paper describes how protein kinase A attaches to CFTR through a linking protein called ezrin.⁠9 Another reports that a metabolic sensor called AMP-activated protein kinase blocks CFTR function.⁠11 One paper reports that carrying a mutation in the CFTR gene can increase susceptibility to chronic rhinosinusitis in the general population.⁠14 Another examines how a common CFTR mutation called DeltaF508 and a genetic variant called the 5T allele appear in patients with chronic pancreatitis and pancreatic cancer.⁠19 One paper finds that diagnosing cystic fibrosis through newborn screening prevents severe malnutrition and improves long-term growth.⁠21 Another paper looks at how a region of DNA upstream of the CFTR gene is regulated.⁠4 One paper examines CFTR gene expression in a different kidney disease called autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease.⁠16 A further paper investigates how a gene involved in immune recognition, called mannose binding lectin, influences the severity of liver disease in cystic fibrosis patients.⁠29 One paper conducts a worldwide genetic analysis of the entire CFTR region of the genome.⁠17 Another describes how an accessory protein helps CFTR molecules interact with each other to boost chloride channel activity.⁠15 A further paper reports that splicing factors can cause part of the CFTR gene to be skipped during processing.⁠8 Another paper studies how a structural region of CFTR called the PDZ-interacting domain is needed for the protein to reach its proper place in the cell membrane.⁠10 One paper describes how a protein called E3KARP links ezrin and protein kinase A to CFTR in airway cells.⁠12 Another examines how two structural parts of CFTR, called nucleotide binding domains, have different roles in how the protein works.⁠5 From the paper "Regulation of cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator single-channel gating by bivalent PDZ-domain-mediated interaction": One paper studies how paired PDZ-domain interactions regulate the opening and closing of the CFTR channel.⁠23 Another describes the structural basis for how a regulatory factor called NHERF1 connects to the tail end of the CFTR protein.⁠28 A further paper examines how often the S1235R mutation appears in CFTR and its clinical significance.⁠26 One paper investigates CFTR mutations and the 5T allele in patients with allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis.⁠27 A further paper examines CFTR gene patterns in Japanese patients with chronic pancreatitis.⁠20 One paper studies the frequency of CFTR mutations found through newborn screening when a digestive enzyme called trypsin is abnormally high.⁠24
